Football player undervaluation model with a RAG scouting assistant

Python, scikit-learn, PyTorch, LangChain, LangGraph, PostgreSQL, Docker, AWS

The question: which players who moved from nine leagues outside the top five into a top five club were undervalued at the time, judged only on what was known before the move?

  • 1,669 usable pre transfer records in PostgreSQL. I started from 1,725 moves and dropped 56 with no appearances in the prior year.
  • Logistic regression, gradient boosting and a PyTorch MLP all beat a constant baseline. They beat a simple minutes rule by only about 0.03 to 0.04 AUC, so I do not claim they are clearly better.
  • The shortlist cuts a league of 170 to 300 eligible players to 100 names. In a backtest, the top 20% by predicted probability became regular starters 56.7% of the time, against a 30.9% base rate.
  • A LangGraph and LangChain agent routes scouting questions to stats or reports. On a held out set of 10 questions it answered 9 correctly, and 10 of 10 on the questions I tuned on. Ten questions is a small test and the README says how wide that interval is.
  • I added computer vision on event maps. It did not improve accuracy on the main benchmark, and it only helped on questions that could be answered from the maps alone. The write up includes how often the vision tags disagreed with the event data.
  • Packaged in Docker and run on a small AWS server.

Premier League match prediction pipeline

Python, PySpark, scikit-learn, GitHub Actions

A pipeline that refreshes fixtures, form and standings on a schedule and publishes predictions to a live dashboard. A weekly job rebuilds the PySpark features, reruns the validation and writes new predictions every Monday.

  • Form features from each team's previous 5 matches, built in PySpark so a match never sees its own result.
  • Logistic regression and gradient boosting, validated season by season, always training on earlier seasons only. Five test seasons from 2021/22 to 2025/26.
  • Logistic regression scored between 47.5% and 54.7% a season, 49.9% over all five. Always picking the home team scored between 41.1% and 48.3%, 44.2% over all five.
  • With 375 matches a season, one standard error is about 2.5 points, so most season to season differences are noise. A simple weighted model I wrote by hand is roughly as good.

Toronto housing and shelter forecasting pipeline

dbt Core, DuckDB, Python, statsmodels

Do Bank of Canada rate changes show up in Toronto shelter demand? I built a tested dbt and DuckDB warehouse from five public sources (a 433K row fact table with 26 automated tests) and looked at the link.

  • In the regression, occupancy rose about 0.64 percentage points for every 1% rate increase, roughly six months later.
  • The shelter system sits near 98% full, so a plain "same as last period" forecast is very hard to beat. SARIMA and Prophet did not beat it.
  • I also tested a monthly model with the policy rate as an input, lagged 6 months. On the held out period it did not reduce error: it was 0.6% worse than the naive forecast, with a mean absolute error of 0.425 percentage points against 0.422.
Code and results

A null result, written up as one. The system being full is the finding.

Toronto shelter overdose spatial analysis

Python, geopandas, PySAL, scikit-learn

A companion to the housing project. What predicts where overdoses happen across Toronto's 158 neighbourhoods?

  • Low income prevalence explained most of the apparent geographic clustering.
  • A machine learning layer cut cross validated error from about 52 to about 33 incidents per neighbourhood.
  • Density based clustering found 6 clusters at settings I fixed before running it. Across a grid of settings it found anywhere from 1 to 9, so the count depends on the settings and I do not treat it as a fixed number. Local Moran's I found one contiguous hotspot group of 9 neighbourhoods.
  • About 84% of incidents could be geocoded. The rest are left out and the README says so.
